# Product

## Intent

Show citizens what their representatives do, from official records, in plain
language — so that judging a politician starts from acts rather than speeches
and promises.

The name is deliberately wider than votes: a source is anything officially
recorded and attributable to a person or a group. Votes are the first one.

## Sources, in order of arrival

1. Assemblée nationale public votes (scrutins) — v1.
2. Find my deputy from a commune or address.
3. Amendments tabled (who proposed what) — often more telling than votes.
   On the deputy page since 06/10/2026.
4. Senate votes, since the October 2023 renewal. On senator and Senate
   scrutin pages since 06/10/2026.
5. HATVP declarations of interests, linked to each deputy.

## Who else does this

- **Datan.fr** — the closest: deputy and group pages, explained votes, loyalty
  to the group, participation. on-record must not be a Datan clone; its angle
  is the multi-source record of a person, and plain-language explanations of
  the votes that matter.
- **NosDéputés.fr** — historical reference, no longer developed for the 17th
  legislature (last code commit July 2024).
- **HowTheyVote.eu** — the European Parliament equivalent, open source; worth
  reading for how it explains votes.

## Editorial principles

These are product rules, not style: a screen that breaks one is a bug.

1. **Only nominal votes exist in the data.** Most votes in the chamber are by
   show of hands and leave no individual trace. Every page that counts votes
   says so.
2. **Absence is not idleness.** Deputies sit in committees while the chamber
   votes. No "absenteeism" ranking, ever. Participation can be shown, with
   that context next to it. In the Senate, where a group may vote for all
   its members, a recorded ballot proves no presence: no participation
   figure at all.
3. **A vote on an amendment is not a vote on an idea.** The page always says
   what was voted on: the whole text, an article, an amendment, a motion of
   censure — and what adopting or rejecting it meant.
4. **Corrections count.** A deputy's "mise au point" (declared intended vote)
   is shown beside the recorded vote, never hidden.
5. **Group at the date of the vote**, not the current group.
6. **Every figure is traceable** to the list of votes it is computed from, and
   every page cites the official source with a link.
7. **No editorial selection without disclosure.** Any curated list ("key
   votes", topics) states who chose it and on what criteria.
